If dynamics carry information that static connectivity does not, then clinical populations are where that
should matter most. Symptoms are rarely explained by damage to one region; they emerge from how the whole
system reorganises.

Work in this line has covered:

- **Multiple sclerosis.** Temporally defined network activation associated with slowed information processing
  speed; disrupted working-memory network dynamics; the relationship between atrophy, lesion load and MEG
  power; and whether altered transient dynamics reflect pathology or treatment.
- **Parkinson's disease.** Competing spectral states in the subthalamic nucleus and their link to motor
  impairment, and how dopaminergic medication modulates spontaneous cortico-subthalamic activity.
- **Stroke.** What determines whether a brain network recovers its organisation after a lesion.
- **Ageing and dementia.** How resting-state cortical networks change with age, whether age acts as moderator
  or confound in brain–cognition relationships, and — the focus of the ATRAE-funded project at CRM —
  integrating imaging modalities to find early markers of Alzheimer's disease.

The ATRAE project, _"A new integrative statistical framework to connect symptoms to mechanisms in brain
disease,"_ treats different neuroimaging modalities as complementary views of one underlying process rather
than as separate analyses to be run in parallel. The aim is a framework in which a clinical measurement and
a mechanistic model can be compared on the same terms.
